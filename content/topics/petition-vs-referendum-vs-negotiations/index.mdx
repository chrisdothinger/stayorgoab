## Short answer

These are separate steps: a petition can trigger process, a referendum can express a democratic result, and negotiations would be a later constitutional/political stage.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

A petition, a referendum, and negotiations are separate stages. Success at one stage can trigger or strengthen the case for the next, but it does not automatically complete the full independence path.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case is escalation: a valid petition and clear referendum can turn a political demand into a mandate for negotiations.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is category error: signatures or a vote cannot themselves settle constitutional amendment, assets, debt, borders, rights, or recognition.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium; the distinction is clear, but current process details can change.
