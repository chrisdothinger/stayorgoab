## Short answer

Public-health surveillance and outbreak response currently span federal, provincial, laboratory, immunization, reporting, and data-sharing systems; independence would require explicit continuity plans for public-health powers, emergency coordination, vaccine-safety reporting, and cross-border public-health alerts.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current sources show federal public-health and vaccine-safety baselines plus Alberta public-health law and immunization-policy baselines; they do not settle how Alberta would maintain surveillance feeds, outbreak notices, vaccine adverse-event reporting, laboratory coordination, or cross-border health alerts after independence.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** Alberta could design a public-health system more accountable to Alberta voters, keep provincial disease-control capacity, and negotiate data-sharing and response agreements that preserve necessary federal and international links while allowing local policy choices.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** Disease surveillance and outbreak response depend on fast, trusted data exchange across borders; even short gaps in legal authority, reporting channels, vaccine-safety feeds, laboratory coordination, or federal public-health relationships could create avoidable risk.
- **Uncertainty label:** High; outcomes depend on negotiated public-health data sharing, laboratory and surveillance agreements, notifiable-disease rules, emergency powers, vaccine procurement and safety reporting, health-workforce capacity, and recognition by Canadian and international public-health networks.

## Why people may share or argue

Public health became highly personal and politically charged after COVID-19; people may argue over whether independence would increase local control or weaken disease surveillance, outbreak coordination, vaccine confidence, and cross-border health warnings.
