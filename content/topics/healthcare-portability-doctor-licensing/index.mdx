## Short answer

Current sources show that insured-health-service principles, Alberta health insurance administration, and physician registration/licensing are separate but connected baselines; independence would require explicit continuity plans rather than assumptions.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current sources show that insured-health-service principles, Alberta health insurance administration, and physician registration/licensing are separate but connected baselines; they do not settle whether an independent Alberta would preserve reciprocal coverage, doctor mobility, billing rules, specialist referrals, or emergency/out-of-province care without new agreements.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro-independence case is that Alberta could maintain universal public coverage in provincial law while designing licensing, workforce, and patient-access agreements around Alberta’s needs, including clearer local accountability for physician supply and health administration.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that healthcare depends on trust and continuity: coverage portability, physician credentials, billing, emergency care, referrals, drug plans, and interprovincial patient movement could become uncertain if Alberta left Canadian legal and funding frameworks before replacement agreements were enforceable.
- **Uncertainty label:** High; healthcare continuity depends on legislation, funding, reciprocal billing, professional licensing, workforce supply, hospitals, emergency care, drugs, and agreements with Canada and other provinces.

## Why people may share or argue

Healthcare is personal and high-trust; people may argue over whether independence could protect Alberta care from Ottawa or risk coverage gaps, physician mobility problems, and uncertainty for snowbirds, students, workers, and patients needing out-of-province treatment.
