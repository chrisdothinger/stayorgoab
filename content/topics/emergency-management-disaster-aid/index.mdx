## Short answer

Emergency response already spans provincial and federal systems; independence would require evidence about command roles, mutual aid, funding, disaster recovery, alerts, and continuity for affected communities.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current public records show federal emergency-management and disaster-financial-assistance roles alongside Alberta emergency-management responsibilities, but they do not establish how command structures, recovery funding, mutual aid, alerts, or federal-provincial support would operate after independence.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** Alberta could seek emergency-management arrangements tailored to provincial hazards, local command structures, and faster recovery decisions.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** Residents and municipalities could face transition risk if disaster funding, mutual aid, federal support, alerting systems, or response roles were unclear during fires, floods, severe weather, or other emergencies.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium; command structures, disaster-recovery funding, mutual aid, federal support replacement, emergency alerts, staffing, equipment, and continuity during fires, floods, severe weather, and other emergencies remain conditional on future decisions and evidence.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.
