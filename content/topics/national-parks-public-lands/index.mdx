## Short answer

Land questions would require separating Alberta public-land powers from federal national-park responsibilities, Indigenous rights, conservation obligations, and any negotiated asset or authority transfer.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current public records show federal national-park and natural-resource institutions alongside Alberta public-land management, but they do not settle ownership, conservation authority, Indigenous consultation, access rules, or asset-transfer terms after independence.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** Alberta could seek more integrated control over land-use decisions, resource policy, recreation access, and conservation priorities inside its territory.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** National parks, federal lands, conservation duties, treaty rights, access rules, and asset transfers could become legally and politically contentious without negotiated authority and source-backed transition plans.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium; ownership or control of federal lands and parks, conservation obligations, Indigenous rights and consultation, public access, staffing, asset transfers, and cross-border land-management agreements remain conditional on future decisions and evidence.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.
