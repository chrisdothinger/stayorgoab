## Short answer

These questions depend on negotiations and future policy choices; the initial page is a transparent seed overview.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Borders, currency, trade, and citizenship would not be automatic consequences of a referendum. They would depend on negotiated arrangements and later policy design.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case is policy choice: Alberta could seek open movement, practical continuity, and institutions tailored to Alberta interests.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is household and business uncertainty around banking, citizenship status, market access, border administration, and continuity of rights.
- **Uncertainty label:** High; these systems depend on counterparties and negotiated policy choices.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.

## What to watch

- Official source updates.
- Court, legislative, fiscal, treaty, or policy changes.
- New credible expert analysis.
- Claims that need source support before they should be treated as settled.
