## Short answer

Air travel depends on federal aviation safety regulation, airport systems, carriers, security screening, and international recognition; independence would need a credible continuity plan.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current public records show federal aviation safety and transportation-regulator roles alongside Alberta transportation infrastructure responsibilities, but they do not establish how aviation safety certification, airport operations, air-service agreements, security, or passenger protections would operate after independence.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** Alberta could seek transport policy and infrastructure priorities tailored to its airports, northern access, trade corridors, and regional connectivity.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** Airlines, airports, passengers, cargo shippers, and insurers could face uncertainty if safety certification, regulator recognition, security screening, international air agreements, or passenger-protection rules were not continuous.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium; aviation safety authority, airport operations, security screening, international air-service agreements, regulator recognition, passenger protections, insurance, and continuity for carriers and travellers remain conditional on future decisions and evidence.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.
