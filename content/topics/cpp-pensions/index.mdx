## Short answer

CPP withdrawal has a statutory path, but transfer amount, timing, and independence-related pension treatment remain materially uncertain.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

CPP withdrawal has a statutory reference point, but independence adds separate questions about benefit continuity, transfer calculations, administration, and negotiations.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case is control: Alberta could argue for pension rules, contribution rates, and investment choices better matched to Alberta workers and demographics.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is household risk: pensions require administrative continuity, federal calculations, public trust, and certainty that current sources do not yet settle.
- **Uncertainty label:** High for benefit design and transfer amount; medium for ordinary CPP-withdrawal mechanics.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.

## What to watch

- Official source updates.
- Court, legislative, fiscal, treaty, or policy changes.
- New credible expert analysis.
- Claims that need source support before they should be treated as settled.
