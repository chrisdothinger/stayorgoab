## Short answer

Treaty rights, section 35 rights, land, and consultation issues are high-risk and require careful source-backed treatment.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Treaty rights, section 35 rights, land, jurisdiction, consultation, and consent are central issues. They cannot be treated as automatic administrative details.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** A cautious pro case would argue that Alberta could seek renewed agreements with Indigenous governments, while admitting those arrangements cannot be presumed.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest pro-federation case is that existing Crown obligations and constitutional protections cannot be bypassed by a provincial referendum.
- **Uncertainty label:** Very high; treaty, section 35, consent, land, and consultation questions require careful sourcing.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.

## What to watch

- Official source updates.
- Court, legislative, fiscal, treaty, or policy changes.
- New credible expert analysis.
- Claims that need source support before they should be treated as settled.
