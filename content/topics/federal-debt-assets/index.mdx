## Short answer

Debt and asset allocation would likely be negotiated and cannot be reduced to a single confirmed number from the sparse record.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Federal debt, assets, liabilities, contracts, and property would likely be negotiated rather than allocated by a simple public number on day one.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case is that Alberta could argue for a favourable allocation using contribution, population, geography, or asset-location principles.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that Canada, creditors, and counterparties may expect Alberta to assume liabilities, and disputes could complicate transition and recognition.
- **Uncertainty label:** High; no settled allocation formula is established in the current source record.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.

## What to watch

- Official source updates.
- Court, legislative, fiscal, treaty, or policy changes.
- New credible expert analysis.
- Claims that need source support before they should be treated as settled.
