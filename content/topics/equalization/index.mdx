## Short answer

Equalization claims require distinguishing program mechanics, Alberta contributions, federal taxes, and post-independence negotiation assumptions.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Equalization claims need to distinguish program payments, federal taxes, federal transfers, and post-independence fiscal arrangements.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case is that independence could end Alberta participation in federal redistribution and let Alberta redirect fiscal capacity to local priorities.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that equalization is only one part of fiscal federalism; independence could also create new costs, lost transfers, or negotiated liabilities.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium-high; program mechanics are sourceable, but net fiscal effects require broader modelling.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.
