## Short answer

Military and security arrangements would depend on policy choices and negotiations; the sparse topic should not imply settled institutions.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Defence, policing, intelligence, emergency management, border enforcement, and security arrangements would require new Alberta capacity or negotiated continuity.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case is that Alberta could design security institutions around its geography, energy infrastructure, border needs, and public-safety preferences.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that Canada already supplies defence, intelligence, and federal policing capacity that would be costly and complex to replace.
- **Uncertainty label:** High; security institutions and costs are unsettled.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.

## What to watch

- Official source updates.
- Court, legislative, fiscal, treaty, or policy changes.
- New credible expert analysis.
- Claims that need source support before they should be treated as settled.
