## Short answer

The strongest pro case is that Alberta could design security institutions around its geography, energy infrastructure, border needs, and public-safety preferences.

## What current sources support

The current source set for this topic is: scc-secession-reference, clarity-act, alberta-budget-documents-2026. These sources are used to establish the public baseline, not to imply that every future outcome is settled.

For this topic, the safe source-backed statement is: Defence, policing, intelligence, emergency management, border enforcement, and security arrangements would require new Alberta capacity or negotiated continuity.

## What this argument must not overclaim

This is the strongest fair version of the pro-independence argument, not a prediction that the argument will succeed. Any claim that depends on negotiations, counterparties, or future law is labelled as conditional.

The report should not say that independence, federation, recognition, fiscal savings, service continuity, pension outcomes, treaty effects, borders, currency, or debt allocation are automatic unless a cited source directly supports that narrower claim.
