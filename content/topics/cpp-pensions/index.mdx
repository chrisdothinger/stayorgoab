## Short answer

Albertans would not have a settled, automatic answer today. Canada Pension Plan withdrawal has a legal calculation framework, and Alberta has publicly explored an Alberta Pension Plan, but neither source record settles the actual transfer amount, benefit-continuity rules, administration, or what would happen if pension separation were tied to an independence negotiation. [1] [2] [3]

## The debate in plain English

Supporters of pension separation argue that Alberta could seek more control over contributions, benefits, governance, and investment policy, using the CPP withdrawal provisions as a starting point rather than beginning from scratch. [1] [2]

Critics argue that pensions are household-security infrastructure, not just a fiscal asset. CPP records, payments, survivor/disability benefits, employer remittances, and rights for people who worked in multiple provinces would all need clear transition rules before anyone should treat a new system as safer or better. [1] [3]

The honest baseline is narrower: Alberta can debate a different pension arrangement, and there is a statutory CPP-withdrawal reference point, but current checked sources do not prove that Albertans would keep identical CPP benefits, receive lower contributions, or face losses. [1] [2]

## Where the debate turns

- **Transfer amount:** the Chief Actuary material makes the subsection 113(2) calculation central, but a calculation topic is not the same as an agreed final cheque. [1]
- **Continuity for households:** retirees, near-retirees, disabled beneficiaries, survivors, workers, employers, and mobile contributors need payment and record rules, not just a high-level promise.
- **Ordinary CPP withdrawal vs. independence:** provincial CPP withdrawal is one legal frame; secession would add constitutional principles and negotiations that current pension sources do not settle. [1] [3]
- **Governance and trust:** any successor plan would have to show credible administration, investment governance, actuarial assumptions, and dispute resolution.
- **Burden of proof:** pro-separation claims are strongest when framed as “Alberta could seek control,” and weakest when presented as guaranteed gains; pro-CPP claims are strongest when framed as “certainty matters,” and weakest if they imply every change is impossible.
