## Short answer

Current sources show federal legal and regulatory baselines for drug approval, safety, compliance, recalls, and health-product information; independence would require explicit continuity plans for medicine access, safety surveillance, and supply chains.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current sources show federal legal and regulatory baselines for drug approval, safety, compliance, recalls, and health-product information; they do not settle whether an independent Alberta would rely on Canadian approvals, create its own regulator, use mutual recognition, or face medicine-supply disruptions.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro-independence case is that Alberta could negotiate recognition of existing drug approvals, preserve pharmacy supply during transition, and eventually design faster or more locally responsive procurement and regulatory pathways where evidence supports them.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that drug safety is not just purchasing: approvals, labelling, inspections, recalls, adverse-event monitoring, imports, shortages, and cross-border supply chains require trusted legal authority and regulator capacity before patients can rely on continuity.
- **Uncertainty label:** High; outcomes depend on Health Canada arrangements, future Alberta regulator design, supply contracts, import permissions, pharmacy systems, federal-provincial health programs, manufacturers, wholesalers, and recall-data continuity.

## Why people may share or argue

Prescription drugs are personal and urgent; people may argue over whether independence could speed access and local purchasing or risk interruptions in approvals, recalls, pharmacovigilance, imports, shortages, and pharmacy supply chains.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.
