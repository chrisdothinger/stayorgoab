## Short answer

The overall economic effect depends on assumptions about trade, currency, debt, assets, taxation, investment, and negotiated outcomes.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

The overall economic effect would depend on trade, investment, currency, debt, tax policy, resource prices, transition costs, and negotiated market access.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case is that Alberta could align tax, regulatory, resource, and spending decisions more closely with its economic base.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that transition uncertainty, trade friction, institutional duplication, investor risk, and debt allocation could outweigh expected gains.
- **Uncertainty label:** High; outcomes are model-dependent and assumption-heavy.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.

## What to watch

- Official source updates.
- Court, legislative, fiscal, treaty, or policy changes.
- New credible expert analysis.
- Claims that need source support before they should be treated as settled.
