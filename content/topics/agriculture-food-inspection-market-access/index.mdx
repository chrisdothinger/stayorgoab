## Short answer

Agriculture and food systems depend on provincial programs, federal inspection, and export-market rules; independence would require source-backed plans for inspection authority and market access.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current public records show federal food inspection and agriculture programs alongside Alberta agriculture responsibilities, but they do not establish how inspection authority, export certification, or market-access arrangements would operate after independence.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case is that Alberta could try to tailor agriculture policy, inspection priorities, and trade promotion more closely to provincial producers.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that producers, processors, exporters, and consumers could face uncertainty if inspection authority, export certification, disease response, or market-recognition arrangements changed before replacements were accepted.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium; food inspection authority, export certification, animal and plant health rules, market recognition, producer support, trade access, and emergency response capacity remain conditional on future decisions and evidence.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.
