## Short answer

The neutral reading is that what would happen to employment insurance and federal income benefits? remains a transition question, not a settled result. The checked sources establish current federal/provincial baselines, but they do not prove final independence terms for benefit eligibility, funding, contribution records, payment continuity, appeal rights, service delivery, and replacement program design [1], [2], [3].

## What current sources support

The checked-in source pack for this topic is: employment-social-development-canada, service-canada-benefits, alberta-income-support. The report uses those records as a bounded source set and does not treat them as a complete forecast.

The safe source-backed statement is: Current public records show federal employment and benefit systems alongside Alberta income-support programs, but they do not establish whether EI, federal benefits, eligibility rules, contribution funding, or service delivery would continue unchanged after independence.

## Core argument

This synthesis is a mediator between the pro report and the anti report, not a third campaign position. The pro-independence report is strongest when it asks whether Alberta could negotiate more direct control while preserving continuity. The anti-independence / pro-federation report is strongest when it asks whether the plan has enough source-backed detail to protect people during transition. A neutral answer should therefore rank claims by source strength: current official systems first, legal process constraints second, and political promises last.

## What is known

- The topic is in the public index as `full_dossier` and is tracked as medium-sensitivity.
- The checked-in sources establish a current public baseline for this question, not a complete settlement of independence terms [1], [2], [3].
- Claims should distinguish official or primary records from inference, advocacy, or forecast.

## What is disputed

- Whether the cited baseline is enough to support stronger political claims being made.
- Whether independence would reduce or increase uncertainty around benefit eligibility, funding, contribution records, payment continuity, appeal rights, service delivery, and replacement program design.
- Which institutions would decide unresolved points, and on what timeline.
- How much weight to give to familiar current arrangements compared with proposed alternatives.

## Assumptions

- Current source records remain accurate until a scheduled source check records an update.
- The report does not assume automatic independence, automatic federal consent, automatic recognition, or automatic continuity of programs.
- Campaign-style claims need corroboration before they become site conclusions.

## Strongest evidence

The strongest evidence is the bounded source pack itself. It contains public records identifying current legal, fiscal, administrative, process, or stakeholder baselines for this topic. Those records keep the report anchored to documents a reader can inspect, rather than slogans or private modelling.

## Weak points

The neutral view is weak if it becomes mushy balance. Some claims are better sourced than others. The mediator role is to compare the pro report and anti report against the same source record, not to split every difference down the middle.

Another weak point is source coverage. The current pack is enough to justify adding the question and explaining uncertainty, but not enough to declare final design, cost, legal authority, implementation date, or public approval.

## Counterarguments

Readers should compare both reports by asking which claims are directly sourced, which are labelled inferences, and which are still political promises or risk assumptions.
