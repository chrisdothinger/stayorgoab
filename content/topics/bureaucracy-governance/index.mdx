## Short answer

An independent state would need institutional capacity; costs and timing are not settled by current sparse records.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

An independent Alberta would need institutions now handled partly or wholly by Canada, including foreign affairs, customs, immigration, tax administration, defence, and financial regulation.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case is institutional responsiveness: Alberta could build public institutions directly accountable to Alberta voters.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is operational risk: building national institutions is expensive, time-consuming, and difficult during a political transition.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium-high; institutional categories are knowable, but cost and timeline are not settled.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.

## What to watch

- Official source updates.
- Court, legislative, fiscal, treaty, or policy changes.
- New credible expert analysis.
- Claims that need source support before they should be treated as settled.
