## Core argument

This synthesis is a mediator between the pro report and the anti report, not a third campaign position. The pro-independence report is strongest when it asks whether Alberta could write continuity into its own laws and institutions. The anti-independence / pro-federation report is strongest when it asks whether source-backed guarantees exist before voters rely on them. The neutral answer should rank current official baselines first, written transition plans second, and political promises last.

For this side, the strongest claim is not that uncertainty disappears. The strongest claim is that readers should evaluate concrete transition questions: who has authority, what agreements are needed, what rights or services must continue during negotiations, what money and staffing are required, and what official evidence would be enough to rely on.

## Strongest evidence

The strongest evidence is the bounded source pack itself. It identifies current institutions, laws, and public responsibilities a transition would touch. That helps readers avoid two common errors: treating independence as if existing systems automatically vanish overnight, or treating existing systems as if they automatically continue without legal authority, agreements, budgets, and operational planning.

For a civic reader, the evidence matters because people need to know what changes in ordinary life would be governed by official records rather than promises. A strong argument should point to the specific functions now visible in the source pack and explain how those functions would be preserved, replaced, or renegotiated.

## Weak points

The weak point is source coverage. The current pack is sufficient to identify the topic and set a baseline, but it is not sufficient to declare final design, cost, implementation date, public approval, court outcome, or recognition by other governments.

This side also weakens if it compares an ideal future against an imperfect present without matching evidence. The fair comparison is between source-backed present arrangements and source-backed transition plans.

## Counterarguments

A fair reader should ask the other side's best question. If reading the pro-independence case, ask whether promised control has a documented continuity plan. If reading the anti-independence / pro-federation case, ask whether continuity concerns are specific to this source record or just generalized fear of change.

The answer should not be a slogan. It should identify which claims are directly sourced, which are labelled inferences, and which still depend on negotiations, legislation, courts, regulators, markets, staffing, technology, recognition, or other governments.
