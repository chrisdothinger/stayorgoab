## Short answer

Current sources show separate federal and Alberta baselines for banks, deposit insurance, prudential supervision, and credit-union deposit guarantees; independence would require explicit continuity plans for depositor protection and financial-stability backstops.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current sources show separate federal and Alberta baselines for banks, deposit insurance, prudential supervision, and credit-union deposit guarantees; they do not settle whether an independent Alberta would retain CDIC coverage, create a new backstop, negotiate transitional coverage, or face confidence risks during a banking transition.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro-independence case is that Alberta could negotiate depositor-protection continuity, preserve ordinary bank services during transition, and design credit-union and financial-sector rules around provincial priorities while seeking practical arrangements with Canadian institutions.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that bank confidence depends on credible legal authority, supervision, lender-of-last-resort arrangements, deposit insurance, payment systems, and public trust; none of those should be assumed to continue without written agreements and institutional capacity.
- **Uncertainty label:** High; deposit protection and financial stability depend on Canada-Alberta negotiations, federal bank law, regulator recognition, payment-system access, central-bank choices, credit-union arrangements, market confidence, and transition timing.

## Why people may share or argue

People may argue over whether savings, payroll accounts, mortgages, credit-union deposits, and bank confidence would be safer under local control or exposed to transition risk without federal deposit insurance and prudential backstops.
