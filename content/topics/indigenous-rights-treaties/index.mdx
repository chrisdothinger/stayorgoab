## Short answer

The SCC reference says Aboriginal interests would need to be taken into account in negotiations; Alberta-specific effects remain high-risk and contested.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Indigenous rights and treaties are not side issues. The Supreme Court says Aboriginal interests would need to be taken into account in secession negotiations, while Alberta-specific effects remain contested.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** A careful pro-independence case would argue that new nation-to-nation arrangements could be negotiated, but only if treaty and rights issues are treated as central rather than incidental.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest pro-federation case is that constitutional rights, treaty relationships, land, consent, and consultation cannot be bypassed by a provincial vote or treated as settled by Alberta alone.
- **Uncertainty label:** Very high; rights, treaties, land, consultation, and litigation are legally and politically sensitive.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.
