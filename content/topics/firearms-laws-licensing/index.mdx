## Short answer

Current sources show firearms licensing and regulatory baselines involving federal law, the RCMP Canadian Firearms Program, and Alberta’s Chief Firearms Office; independence would require explicit continuity plans rather than assumptions.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current sources show firearms licensing and regulatory baselines involving federal law, the RCMP Canadian Firearms Program, and Alberta’s Chief Firearms Office; they do not settle whether an independent Alberta would keep, replace, relax, tighten, or renegotiate firearms classifications, licensing, enforcement, data systems, or import controls.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro-independence case is that Alberta could write firearms policy closer to provincial priorities, rural realities, sport-shooting communities, and local accountability, instead of relying on federal policy choices that many Alberta gun owners dispute.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that firearms regulation is a public-safety and cross-border enforcement system: licensing records, prohibited and restricted classifications, imports, tracing, police checks, safe storage, and mutual recognition could create safety and legal gaps if continuity were not explicit.
- **Uncertainty label:** High; firearms rules connect criminal law, licensing databases, policing, import controls, public safety, courts, and politically contested rights-and-safety claims.

## Why people may share or argue

Firearms rules are politically charged, especially around rural safety, sport shooting, public safety, federal bans, and provincial-federal conflict; people may argue over whether independence would restore local control or weaken safety safeguards.
