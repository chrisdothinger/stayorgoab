## Short answer

The neutral reading is that public-service continuity depends on fiscal capacity, staffing, legislation, and
transition planning; sparse claims must avoid certainty. The current record supports a bounded analysis, not a
confident prediction: fiscal capacity, staffing, law, delivery systems, and transition timing remain
conditional on future decisions and evidence [1], [2], [3].

## Core argument

This synthesis compares the pro and anti cases rather than creating a third campaign position. The pro-independence side
generally points to control, mandate, leverage, or policy flexibility. The anti-independence/pro-federation side generally points to
transition risk, unresolved legal or fiscal questions, and the need for proof before replacing existing
arrangements. For this topic, the decisive issue is not whether one side can imagine a better or worse future;
it is whether source-backed evidence has narrowed uncertainty around fiscal capacity, staffing, law, delivery
systems, and transition timing.

## What is disputed

- Whether the cited baseline is enough to support the stronger political claim being made.
- Whether independence would reduce or increase the uncertainty around fiscal capacity, staffing, law, delivery systems, and transition timing.
- Which institutions would decide the unresolved points, and on what timeline.
- How much weight to give to familiar current arrangements compared with proposed alternatives.

## Assumptions

- Current source records remain accurate until a scheduled source check records an update.
- The report does not assume automatic independence, automatic federal consent, automatic recognition, or automatic continuity of programs.
- The report treats campaign-style claims as needing corroboration before they become site conclusions.
- If a future source directly addresses this topic, the assessment should be revised rather than stretched from adjacent sources.

## Strongest evidence

The strongest evidence is the bounded source pack itself. It contains public records that identify the current
legal, fiscal, process, or stakeholder baseline for this topic. Those records are useful because they keep the
report anchored to documents a reader can inspect, rather than to slogans or private modelling.

## Weak points

The neutral view is weak if it becomes mushy balance. Some claims are better supported than others. This
report treats official records and primary legal materials as stronger than campaign assertions, while still
noting that even strong baseline sources may not answer future independence terms.

## Counterarguments

A reader should compare both reports by asking which claims are directly sourced, which are reasonable
inferences, and which are still political promises or risk assumptions.
