## Short answer

A referendum could create a political obligation to negotiate only if the question and result are clear; it would not itself make Alberta independent.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

A referendum can matter politically, but it does not by itself create independence. Current legal sources point to a sequence: democratic expression, clarity assessment, negotiations, and constitutional implementation if terms are agreed.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** A clear vote on a clear question would give independence advocates their strongest democratic mandate and could create a constitutional obligation for other governments to negotiate in good faith.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The same legal sources make unilateral secession a weak claim: the result would still depend on Canada, constitutional actors, Indigenous interests, negotiated terms, and implementation law.
- **Uncertainty label:** High for outcomes; medium for the basic legal sequence.
