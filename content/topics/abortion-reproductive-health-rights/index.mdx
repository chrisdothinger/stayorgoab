## Short answer

Abortion and reproductive healthcare currently sit within Canadian criminal-law, constitutional, health-system, and provincial-service baselines; independence would require explicit legal and service-continuity commitments rather than assumptions.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current sources show that abortion access is shaped by the post-Morgentaler criminal-law baseline, federal health-system principles, and Alberta service delivery; they do not settle how an independent Alberta would protect, fund, regulate, restrict, or expand reproductive-health services.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro-independence case is that Alberta could choose to maintain or clarify access in its own laws and health system, potentially making service rules more transparent if continuity were written into transition legislation.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that reproductive healthcare is a rights-and-access issue where legal uncertainty, funding changes, provider shortages, regional access gaps, or changed political control could affect people quickly if continuity were not explicit and enforceable.
- **Uncertainty label:** High; the legal baseline, health funding, provider licensing, facility access, conscience rules, court remedies, and future political choices would all affect reproductive-health access.

## Why people may share or argue

This is one of the most sensitive healthcare and rights topics in Canadian politics. People may share or argue about it because independence raises immediate fears or assurances about bodily autonomy, access in rural areas, doctors, hospitals, and whether current Canadian legal protections would still matter.
