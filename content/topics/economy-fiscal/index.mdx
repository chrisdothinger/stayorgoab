## Short answer

Alberta has a published fiscal plan, resource-revenue exposure, and debt projections; independence claims require assumptions that are not settled by current official documents.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Alberta has a current fiscal baseline, but that baseline does not answer the independence question by itself. Independence scenarios depend on negotiated costs, revenue changes, and institutional choices.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case is fiscal control: Alberta could keep more policy authority over taxes, resource revenue, regulation, and spending priorities.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is transition exposure: debt allocation, duplicated institutions, service costs, market reaction, and trade uncertainty could offset claimed fiscal gains.
- **Uncertainty label:** High; outcomes depend on assumptions about revenue, trade, debt, institutions, and transition costs.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.

## What to watch

- Official source updates.
- Court, legislative, fiscal, treaty, or policy changes.
- New credible expert analysis.
- Claims that need source support before they should be treated as settled.
