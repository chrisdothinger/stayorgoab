## Short answer

Statistics, census, and public data underpin funding formulas, elections, business planning, research, and public accountability; independence would require credible data-governance continuity and comparability.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current public records show federal statistical and census systems alongside Alberta public-data products, but they do not establish how a new statistical agency, census authority, privacy rules, data sharing, historical comparability, or official indicators would operate after independence.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** Alberta could seek a statistical system focused on provincial priorities, faster local data products, and metrics designed around Alberta policy decisions.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** Governments, businesses, researchers, municipalities, and residents could lose comparability or reliability if census authority, data sharing, survey capacity, privacy rules, or official indicators were not continuous.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium; census authority, statistical-agency design, survey capacity, privacy rules, data sharing, historical comparability, official indicators, and continuity for governments, businesses, researchers, municipalities, and residents remain conditional on future decisions and evidence.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.
