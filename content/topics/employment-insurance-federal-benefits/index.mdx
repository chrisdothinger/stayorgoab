## Short answer

Employment Insurance and federal income benefits are currently administered through federal systems; independence would require evidence about continuity, eligibility, funding, and replacement administration.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current public records show federal employment and benefit systems alongside Alberta income-support programs, but they do not establish whether EI, federal benefits, eligibility rules, contribution funding, or service delivery would continue unchanged after independence.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** Alberta could try to design income-security programs and labour-market supports around provincial conditions and integrate them with provincial service delivery.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** Workers and households could face uncertainty if EI, federal benefits, appeals, records, contribution histories, or payment systems changed before replacement agreements and administration were ready.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium; benefit eligibility, funding, contribution records, payment continuity, appeal rights, service delivery, and replacement program design remain conditional on future decisions and evidence.
