## Short answer

Current sources show federal SIN administration and federal/provincial privacy-law baselines; independence would require explicit rules for identity numbers, data transfers, public-sector records, private-sector privacy, and service continuity.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current sources show federal SIN administration and federal/provincial privacy-law baselines; they do not settle whether an independent Alberta would keep SINs for transitional purposes, issue new identifiers, inherit federal records, or negotiate cross-border data-sharing arrangements.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro-independence case is that Alberta could write clear privacy and identity-continuity rules, protect existing service access during transition, and design a provincial digital-government model with stronger local accountability if it is backed by statute and implementation detail.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that identity systems touch taxes, payroll, benefits, banking, immigration, health records, education, law enforcement, and private-sector compliance; confusion over numbers, data custody, consent, and jurisdiction could affect ordinary services quickly.
- **Uncertainty label:** High; outcomes depend on Canada-Alberta agreements, future Alberta privacy statutes, records-transfer rules, public-sector system design, private-sector compliance, benefits and tax administration, banking requirements, and cybersecurity capacity.

## Why people may share or argue

Identity and privacy issues feel immediate: people may argue over payroll, tax filing, benefits, banking, health records, surveillance, data transfers, and whether independence would strengthen local control or create confusion and exposure.
