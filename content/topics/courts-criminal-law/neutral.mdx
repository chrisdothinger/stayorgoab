## Short answer

The neutral reading is that what would happen to courts, criminal law, and the justice system? remains a transition question, not a settled result. The checked sources establish current federal/provincial baselines and legal process constraints, but they do not prove final independence terms for criminal-law authority, court continuity, prosecutions, appeals, custody, enforcement, and transitional validity of existing orders [1], [2], [3].

## What current sources support

The checked-in source pack for this topic is: justice-canada-criminal-justice, alberta-courts, scc-secession-reference. The report uses those records as a bounded source set and does not treat them as a complete forecast.

The safe source-backed statement is: Current public records show existing federal criminal-justice context and Alberta court services, but they do not settle how laws, prosecutions, courts, appeals, custody, or enforcement would transition under independence.

## Core argument

This synthesis is a mediator between the pro report and the anti report, not a third campaign position. The pro-independence report is strongest when it asks whether Alberta could negotiate more direct control while preserving continuity. The anti-independence / pro-federation report is strongest when it asks whether the plan has enough source-backed detail to protect people during transition. A neutral answer should therefore rank claims by source strength: current official systems first, legal process constraints second, and political promises last.

## Assumptions

- Current source records remain accurate until a scheduled source check records an update.
- The report does not assume automatic independence, automatic federal consent, automatic recognition, or automatic continuity of programs.
- The report treats campaign-style claims as needing corroboration before they become site conclusions.
- If a future source directly addresses this topic, the assessment should be revised rather than stretched from adjacent sources.

## Strongest evidence

The strongest evidence is the bounded source pack itself. It contains public records that identify the current legal, fiscal, administrative, process, or stakeholder baseline for this topic. Those records are useful because they keep the report anchored to documents a reader can inspect, rather than to slogans or private modelling. They also show what the present system actually does before anyone argues that a different system would be better or worse.

For a civic reader, that matters. Independence debates often compress a large administrative transition into a one-line promise or warning. A source-first dossier should resist that compression. The first question is not whether a future Alberta could imagine an answer; it is what would have to be decided, who would decide it, what current institution would be replaced or continued, and what evidence would show continuity for ordinary people.

## Weak points

The neutral view is weak if it becomes mushy balance. Some claims are simply better sourced than others. The mediator role is to compare the pro report and anti report against the same source record, not to split every difference down the middle.

Another weak point is source coverage. The current pack is enough to justify adding the question and explaining the uncertainty, but not enough to declare final design, cost, legal authority, implementation date, or public approval. If later official plans, legislation, court records, or independent costing emerge, this dossier should be updated instead of treating today's baseline as permanent.

## Counterarguments

Readers should compare both reports by asking which claims are directly sourced, which are labelled inferences, and which are still political promises or risk assumptions.

A fair reader should also ask whether each side is comparing like with like. A polished future promise should not be compared against every flaw of the current system unless the promise has evidence behind it. Likewise, current continuity should not be treated as proof that present arrangements are optimal. The useful comparison is between source-backed present arrangements and source-backed transition plans.
