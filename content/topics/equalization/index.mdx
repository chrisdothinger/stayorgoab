## Short answer

Not a clean cheque. Alberta does not receive equalization, but Albertans do not pay a separate equalization tax that could simply be cancelled and returned dollar-for-dollar. The federal equalization page and major-transfer table treat equalization as one part of federal fiscal arrangements, not as a separate cheque Alberta can simply stop sending. Ending participation in those arrangements could create fiscal room, but how much would show up for Albertans would depend on federal tax changes, lost or changed transfers, replacement of federal services, debt/assets, and any negotiated independence settlement.

## The debate in plain English

The slogan version says Alberta would “save equalization.” The careful version asks three different questions: what equalization pays to recipient provinces; what other federal transfers and spending flow to Alberta; and what taxes, services, debt, assets, and transition costs would change if Alberta left Canada.

Independence supporters focus on fiscal control: Alberta could try to keep more of the money now routed through Ottawa and redirect it to Alberta priorities. Pro-federation critics focus on full accounting: ending one transfer program does not end the need to fund health care, pensions, borders, regulation, defence, debt service, and other public functions.

## Where the debate turns

- Whether the claim is about equalization specifically or the whole federal fiscal relationship.
- Whether Alberta’s current zero-equalization status is separated from other federal transfers, including health and social transfers.
- Whether any proposed savings count lost federal spending and new state responsibilities.
- Whether a secession process is treated as negotiated rather than automatic.
- Whether a dollar figure is sourced to official transfer tables or a credible full fiscal model.
