## Short answer

The Clarity Act sets federal conditions before Canada enters secession negotiations, but Alberta-specific effects require careful legal sourcing.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

The Clarity Act gives the House of Commons a role in assessing whether a referendum question and result are clear enough before Canada enters secession negotiations.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** A pro-independence strategy would try to satisfy the Act with a direct question and a decisive result, making refusal to negotiate politically harder.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** A pro-federation strategy would stress that ambiguity, narrow support, or unclear consequences give federal actors grounds to reject automatic negotiations.
- **Uncertainty label:** High for Alberta-specific application; lower for the existence of the federal clarity framework.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.
