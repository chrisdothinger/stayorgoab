## Short answer

The Supreme Court rejected unilateral provincial secession and described when a duty to negotiate may arise.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

The Supreme Court rejected unilateral provincial secession while recognizing that a clear democratic expression for secession would create a duty to negotiate within the constitutional order.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case points to democratic legitimacy: a clear Alberta vote could not be ignored and would create pressure for good-faith negotiations.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest pro-federation case points to constraint: the Reference does not let a province leave on its own or dictate terms to Canada.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium; the core holding is sourceable, but Alberta-specific implications remain contested.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.
