## Short answer

The strongest pro case is that a lawful, negotiated, clearly democratic process could make recognition more likely and reduce international resistance.

## What current sources support

The current source set for this topic is: scc-secession-reference, clarity-act. These sources are used to establish the public baseline, not to imply that every future outcome is settled.

For this topic, the safe source-backed statement is: International recognition would depend on legality, negotiations with Canada, democratic legitimacy, treatment of rights and treaties, and decisions by other states.

## Strongest pro-independence case

The strongest pro case is that a lawful, negotiated, clearly democratic process could make recognition more likely and reduce international resistance.

## What this argument must not overclaim

This is the strongest fair version of the pro-independence argument, not a prediction that the argument will succeed. Any claim that depends on negotiations, counterparties, or future law is labelled as conditional.

The report should not say that independence, federation, recognition, fiscal savings, service continuity, pension outcomes, treaty effects, borders, currency, or debt allocation are automatic unless a cited source directly supports that narrower claim.

## Main uncertainty

High; recognition depends on external actors and process legitimacy.
