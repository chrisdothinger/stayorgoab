## Short answer

Immigration, passport, citizenship, and movement rights would depend on negotiated legal arrangements and new administrative choices, not on a simple automatic rollover.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current public records place immigration, citizenship, and passport services in federal systems, while the Secession Reference frames secession as a negotiated constitutional process rather than a unilateral administrative switch.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case is that a negotiated independence process could seek mobility continuity and immigration rules tailored to Alberta labour-market needs.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that passport, citizenship, and movement rights are core legal statuses that could become uncertain without detailed agreements with Canada and other states.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium; immigration status, passport eligibility, citizenship continuity, residency rules, and movement across any future border remain conditional on future decisions and evidence.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.
