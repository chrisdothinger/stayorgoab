## Short answer

The strongest anti-independence case is that passport, citizenship, and movement rights are core legal statuses that could become uncertain without detailed agreements with Canada and other states. On this question, the safer source-first conclusion is that independence could create legal, administrative, or service-continuity uncertainty before any claimed benefit is realized [1], [2], [3].

## What current sources support

The checked-in source pack for this topic is: ircc-main, canadian-passports, scc-secession-reference. The report uses those records as a bounded source set and does not treat them as a complete forecast.

The safe source-backed statement is: Current public records place immigration, citizenship, and passport services in federal systems, while the Secession Reference frames secession as a negotiated constitutional process rather than a unilateral administrative switch.

## Core argument

A careful anti case focuses on burden of proof. The current system may be imperfect, but independence would require replacing or renegotiating parts of it. For this topic, the key unresolved area is immigration status, passport eligibility, citizenship continuity, residency rules, and movement across any future border. The checked-in sources support warning readers that current public baselines do not equal settled final arrangements. The anti argument is strongest when it distinguishes confirmed current systems from speculative claims and asks what evidence would be needed before treating independence as lower-risk than remaining in Canada.

## What is disputed

- Whether the cited baseline is enough to support the stronger political claim being made.
- Whether independence would reduce or increase the uncertainty around immigration status, passport eligibility, citizenship continuity, residency rules, and movement across any future border.
- Which institutions would decide unresolved points, and on what timeline.
- How much weight to give to familiar current arrangements compared with proposed alternatives.

- Current source records remain accurate until a scheduled source check records an update.
- The report does not assume automatic independence, automatic federal consent, automatic recognition, or automatic continuity of programs.

## Strongest evidence

The strongest evidence is the bounded source pack itself. It contains public records that identify the current legal, fiscal, administrative, process, or stakeholder baseline for this topic. Those records are useful because they keep the report anchored to documents a reader can inspect, rather than to slogans or private modelling. They also show what the present system actually does before anyone argues that a different system would be better or worse.

For a civic reader, that matters. Independence debates often compress a large administrative transition into a one-line promise or warning. A source-first dossier should resist that compression. The first question is not whether a future Alberta could imagine an answer; it is what would have to be decided, who would decide it, what current institution would be replaced or continued, and what evidence would show continuity for ordinary people.

## Weak points

The anti case becomes weak if it treats all uncertainty as proof that change cannot work. Some uncertainties could be narrowed by clearer plans, direct negotiations, independent costing, official legal positions, or later public records.

Another weak point is source coverage. The current pack is enough to justify adding the question and explaining the uncertainty, but not enough to declare final design, cost, legal authority, implementation date, or public approval. If later official plans, legislation, court records, or independent costing emerge, this dossier should be updated instead of treating today's baseline as permanent.

## Counterarguments

The main pro-independence reply is that uncertainty also exists inside Confederation. Advocates can argue that Alberta should not treat current arrangements as risk-free simply because they are familiar.

A fair reader should also ask whether each side is comparing like with like. A polished future promise should not be compared against every flaw of the current system unless the promise has evidence behind it. Likewise, current continuity should not be treated as proof that present arrangements are optimal. The useful comparison is between source-backed present arrangements and source-backed transition plans.
