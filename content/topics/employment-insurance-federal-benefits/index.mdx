title: Would Albertans keep Employment Insurance and federal income benefits?

## Short answer

Not automatically by promise, and not automatically lost overnight by slogan. Today, Employment Insurance and many income benefits are federal systems, while Alberta also runs provincial income-support programs [1], [2], [3]. If Alberta became independent, continuity would depend on negotiated transition terms, legislation, funding, data transfer, service capacity, and clear public rules for claims already in progress.

## The debate in plain English

The practical question is whether workers, seniors, parents, disabled people, unemployed people, and low-income households would keep getting payments while governments changed legal authority and administration. Current sources show the institutions people use now: federal employment/social-development and benefit portals, plus Alberta income support [1], [2], [3]. They do not contain an independence agreement saying which federal benefits would continue, for how long, who would pay, or how contribution and eligibility records would transfer.

The pro-independence case says Alberta could negotiate continuity first and then build income-security programs that fit Alberta's labour market and provincial service network. The anti-independence / pro-federation case says those payments are too important to treat as a design exercise without signed agreements, tested administration, and appeal protections.

## Where the debate turns

- **Continuity of payments:** would EI and benefit payments continue under a written transition agreement, or would Alberta need a replacement ready on day one?
- **Eligibility and records:** would contribution histories, work records, benefit applications, overpayments, appeals, and identity records be recognized across systems?
- **Funding:** would premiums, taxes, federal transfers, or Alberta replacement revenue cover the same promises without gaps?
- **Administration:** would Service Canada, federal benefit portals, Alberta income-support offices, or a new Alberta agency handle intake, payments, reviews, fraud controls, and appeals [1], [2], [3]?
- **Public proof:** the key evidence would be legislation, agreements, budgets, operational readiness tests, and plain-language instructions to claimants—not campaign assurances.
