# Reader-first dossier writing contract

This is the public writing standard for StayOrGoAB dossiers when a topic is new, migrated, or substantially refreshed.

## Product promise

The agents do the critical thinking. The reader should be able to read the page once and understand the issue without deciphering policy language.

The goal is not to dumb down the work. The goal is to translate hard legal, fiscal, service, and rights questions into clear public language while keeping the evidence trail intact.

## Required public shape

### Plain-English answer first

Start with the answer a normal reader came for. Use short paragraphs. Say what is known, what is not settled, and why the answer is conditional.

### What this means for Albertans

Explain the everyday implication. Name the affected systems directly: pensions, taxes, borders, services, courts, treaty rights, permits, public lands, or whatever the topic actually touches.

### What would have to be decided

Turn abstract complexity into concrete decision points. Prefer questions like:

- Who decides?
- Who pays?
- Which law applies?
- Who runs the system during the transition?
- What has to be negotiated before the promise is real?

### Serious pro and anti briefs

Pro and anti reports remain professional debate briefs. Each side should make its strongest fair case with multiple pillars, real objections, and clear limits. Do not turn either side into a slogan or a campaign ad.

### Neutral as mediator

Neutral is not a third side. It should explain what each side gets right, where each side overstates, what survives both arguments, and what evidence would change the answer.

## Style rules

- Use ordinary words before institutional jargon.
- If a technical term is unavoidable, define it in the same sentence or the next one.
- Prefer concrete actors over abstractions: Alberta, Canada, Parliament, courts, Indigenous nations, voters, residents, businesses.
- Prefer short paragraphs on mobile.
- Keep caveats close to the claim they qualify.
- Avoid legal/policy fog such as "constitutional significance," "rights architecture," "institutional continuity," "lawful escalation," and "jurisdictional uncertainty" unless the sentence immediately explains the plain meaning.
- Do not use filler phrases such as "it is important to note," "at its core," "underscores," "pivotal," or "landscape."
- Do not make the user do the synthesis work. The report should state the practical meaning.

## Citation and audit rule

Do not reduce citation discipline to improve readability. Keep numbered citations, matching `## Sources`, source maps, claim maps, audit logs, and redebate logs intact.

Evidence should stay close to the argument it supports. The sentence should be readable on its own; the citation should let a skeptical reader verify it.

## Reader test

Before publishing, ask:

> Could a normal person understand this on a phone, tired, without rereading it twice?

If the honest answer is no, rewrite the sentence.
