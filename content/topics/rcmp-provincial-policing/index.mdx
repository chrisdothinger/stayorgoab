## Short answer

Policing could be reorganized under an independent Alberta, but today’s RCMP contracts, municipal services, First Nations policing, dispatch, labs, data systems, and transition costs would require written continuity plans rather than assumptions.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current sources show Alberta already manages important policing policy and oversight functions while RCMP contract policing and municipal/Indigenous policing arrangements remain part of the current system; they do not establish what police service, contracts, costs, labour arrangements, databases, labs, or mutual-aid rules would apply after independence.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** Alberta could argue that independence would let it design policing around provincial priorities, rural response times, local accountability, and a single public-safety strategy instead of relying as heavily on federal RCMP arrangements.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that policing is a live public-safety service: unclear contracts, staffing, training, dispatch, lab access, data-sharing, First Nations policing, and court-adjacent enforcement could create real transition risk if continuity were not guaranteed.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium-high; policing is operationally sensitive, partly contractual, and tied to federal RCMP capacity, local police services, municipalities, First Nations communities, courts, labs, information systems, and transition funding.

## Why people may share or argue

Policing is visible in everyday life and is already politically charged in Alberta; people may argue over rural crime, RCMP trust, costs, provincial-police proposals, and whether independence would improve accountability or create dangerous disruption.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.
