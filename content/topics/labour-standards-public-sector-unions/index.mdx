## Short answer

Current sources show federal labour-law baselines for federally regulated work and Alberta baselines for employment standards, labour relations, and bargaining institutions; independence would require explicit continuity rules for workers, employers, unions, and public-sector contracts.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current sources show federal labour-law baselines for federally regulated work and Alberta baselines for employment standards, labour relations, and labour-board administration; they do not settle how an independent Alberta would treat federal-sector workers, existing collective agreements, essential-services rules, pensions, successor rights, or public-sector bargaining mandates.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro-independence case is that Alberta could consolidate workplace regulation under local statutes, tailor labour standards and bargaining rules to Alberta’s economy, and write transition clauses that preserve existing contracts while reducing federal-provincial complexity.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that workers and employers need certainty before legal jurisdiction changes; federal-sector employees, union certifications, public-sector contracts, strike/lockout rules, tribunal files, and interprovincial employers could face real disruption without detailed successor legislation and agreements.
- **Uncertainty label:** High; outcomes depend on transition legislation, labour-board continuity, collective-agreement successor rules, treatment of federally regulated employers, court challenges, public-sector bargaining mandates, fiscal capacity, and negotiated recognition by Canada and other jurisdictions.

## Why people may share or argue

Workplace rules affect paycheques, strikes, public services, oilfield contractors, federal-sector employees, nurses, teachers, civil servants, and employers; people may argue over whether independence would improve local bargaining control or destabilize rights and contracts.
