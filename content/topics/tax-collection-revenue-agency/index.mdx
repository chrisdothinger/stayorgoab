## Short answer

Independence would require clear decisions about who administers taxes, benefits, audits, and taxpayer services; existing federal and provincial systems do not answer that transition by themselves.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current public records show that residents and businesses interact with federal tax services and Alberta tax programs, but they do not establish a ready-made independent Alberta tax agency or transition plan.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case is that a local revenue administration could align tax collection, compliance priorities, and public accountability with Alberta policy choices.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that standing up or replacing tax systems could disrupt filing, benefits, audits, employer remittances, and public revenue during transition.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium; tax administration, benefits delivery, audit authority, data transfer, and taxpayer-service continuity remain conditional on future decisions and evidence.

## How to read the reports
