## Short answer

Post-secondary finance would require separating Alberta institutions and grants from federal student aid and research-funding programs; current sources show today’s systems, not an independence transition plan.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current public records show federal student financial assistance, Alberta student aid, and federal tri-agency research funding, but they do not establish how loans, grants, university transfers, or research programs would be replaced or continued after independence.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case is that more local post-secondary policy, student-aid design, and research priorities could be aligned with Alberta institutions and taxpayers.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that students, universities, researchers, and lenders could face uncertainty if federal aid, grant programs, loan servicing, or institutional funding channels changed before replacement systems were proven.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium; student-loan servicing, grant eligibility, university funding, research councils, data transfer, and transition financing remain conditional on future decisions and evidence.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.
