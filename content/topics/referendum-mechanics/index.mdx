## Short answer

Elections Alberta publishes the petition and referendum process; several steps are procedural and date-sensitive.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Petitions, referendum administration, ballot rules, and official timelines are procedural. They can open democratic decision points but do not resolve legal independence outcomes by themselves.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case is procedural legitimacy: collecting signatures and winning a referendum could show democratic demand for negotiations.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that process compliance is not the same as constitutional success; timing, wording, litigation, and post-vote negotiations still matter.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium; process sources are strong, but dates, litigation, and official pages are time-sensitive.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.

## What to watch

- Official source updates.
- Court, legislative, fiscal, treaty, or policy changes.
- New credible expert analysis.
- Claims that need source support before they should be treated as settled.
