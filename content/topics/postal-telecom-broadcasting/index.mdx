## Short answer

Mail, telecom, spectrum, and broadcasting are currently tied to federal institutions and regulators; independence claims need evidence about continuity, regulation, market access, and consumer protection.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current public records show national postal services, federal communications regulation, and federal spectrum-management functions, but they do not establish how an independent Alberta would maintain mail delivery, carrier regulation, spectrum licences, or broadcasting rules.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case is that local communications policy could be designed around Alberta consumer, rural-connectivity, and business priorities.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that mail delivery, carrier obligations, spectrum rights, consumer protections, emergency communications, and broadcasting rules could become complicated if federal systems were interrupted or renegotiated.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium; postal continuity, telecom regulation, spectrum licensing, broadcasting jurisdiction, consumer protection, emergency communications, and cross-border carrier arrangements remain conditional on future decisions and evidence.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.

## What to watch

- Official source updates.
- Court, legislative, fiscal, treaty, regulator, or policy changes.
- New credible expert analysis.
- Claims that need source support before they should be treated as settled.
