## Short answer

Water governance involves provincial allocation and infrastructure plus federal and interjurisdictional water roles; independence would require clear legal and operational continuity for rivers, drinking water, wastewater, and cross-border water issues.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current public records show Alberta water-management and drinking-water/wastewater responsibilities alongside federal transboundary-water information, but they do not settle how water allocation, river-basin management, water-quality rules, infrastructure funding, Indigenous rights, or interjurisdictional arrangements would operate after independence.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** Alberta could seek more direct control over water allocation, infrastructure priorities, and watershed policy while designing arrangements around local river basins and municipal needs.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** Communities, irrigators, industries, Indigenous governments, and downstream users could face uncertainty if water rights, quality rules, infrastructure funding, or cross-border river arrangements were not continuous.
- **Uncertainty label:** Medium; water allocation, river-basin management, drinking-water and wastewater oversight, infrastructure funding, Indigenous rights, environmental standards, and interjurisdictional water arrangements remain conditional on future decisions and evidence.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.
