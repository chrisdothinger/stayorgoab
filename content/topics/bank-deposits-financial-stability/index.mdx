## Short answer

Not automatically settled. Today, federally regulated banks sit under Canadian bank law, OSFI supervision, CDIC deposit insurance, Bank of Canada stability functions, and Canadian payment-system law, while Alberta credit-union deposits are protected through a provincial credit-union guarantee framework. Independence would not answer by itself whether those protections continue, are replaced, or are bridged by negotiated transition agreements. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

## The debate in plain English

The pro-independence argument is that Alberta could make depositor protection a first-order transition item: keep ordinary bank accounts operating, negotiate continuity with Canadian institutions, preserve confidence for payroll and mortgages, and design any Alberta backstop around local priorities. The case is strongest when it treats continuity as something to legislate, fund, and negotiate, not as something that happens automatically.

The anti-independence / pro-federation argument is that household savings depend on more than a political promise. Deposit insurance, bank supervision, payment-system access, central-bank liquidity, resolution powers, product disclosure, and public confidence all have to be legally credible before depositors rely on them. The current Canadian framework is an operating system; replacing or renegotiating it would be a high-stakes task. [2] [4] [5] [6] [7]

The neutral answer is therefore conditional: deposits and savings could remain protected only if the transition created enforceable coverage, supervision, liquidity, resolution, and payment arrangements before confidence was tested.

## Where the debate turns

- **Coverage authority:** who has legal power to insure bank deposits after independence, and does any Canadian coverage continue during transition? [1] [2]
- **Prudential supervision:** which regulator oversees banks serving Albertans, and would institutions accept that framework? [4]
- **Credit unions:** Alberta’s current credit-union guarantee is a real provincial baseline, but its role in a sovereign-state transition would still need legal and fiscal detail. [7]
- **Backstops beyond insurance:** depositor confidence also depends on liquidity support, bank-resolution powers, payment clearing, and market trust. [5] [6]
- **Product clarity:** insured deposits, credit-union deposits, and investment accounts are not the same thing; public promises need product-specific coverage rules.
- **Timing:** even a good long-term plan can be damaged if account holders, employers, and financial institutions face uncertainty before agreements are public.
