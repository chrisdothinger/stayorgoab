## Short answer

Election administration, parties, candidate rules, and campaign finance currently exist under separate federal and Alberta legal systems; independence would require clear rules for continuity, voter eligibility, party registration, spending limits, third-party advertising, enforcement, and transition elections.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Current sources show separate federal and Alberta baselines for election administration, party/candidate regulation, and campaign finance; they do not settle what rules would govern transition elections, party continuity, spending limits, enforcement, or eligibility after independence.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** Alberta could write a democratic framework suited to its own institutions, preserve Elections Alberta capacity, and design campaign-finance and party rules that voters can judge directly during a transition.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** Election-law transitions are legitimacy-critical; if rules for parties, spending, advertising, boundaries, eligibility, enforcement, or transition elections are unclear or perceived as self-serving, independence could deepen distrust rather than resolve it.
- **Uncertainty label:** High; outcomes depend on transition legislation, constitutional design, electoral boundaries, voter eligibility, party-registration rules, campaign-finance limits, third-party advertising rules, enforcement independence, court review, and public legitimacy.

## Why people may share or argue

Election rules decide who can organize, spend, vote, and govern; people may argue over whether independence would create more accountable local democracy or let incumbents write unfair rules during a high-stakes transition.
