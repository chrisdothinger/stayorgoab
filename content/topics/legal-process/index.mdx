## Short answer

Alberta could not become independent by referendum alone. Current Canadian legal sources point to a sequence: a clear question, a clear democratic expression, negotiations, and constitutional change.

## What we know

The Supreme Court of Canada rejected unilateral provincial secession under Canadian constitutional law. The Clarity Act sets federal clarity conditions before Canada enters negotiations.

## What is disputed

Political actors can disagree about what question is clear, what majority is clear, and what negotiation obligations should mean in practice.

## What remains unknown

The exact negotiating parties, terms, constitutional path, timelines, and settlement of federal-provincial assets and obligations would depend on future events.
