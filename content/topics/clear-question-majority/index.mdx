## Short answer

The meaning of a clear question and clear majority is politically and legally consequential, not a simple fixed percentage in the current sparse record.

## What this dossier covers

This dossier separates the neutral baseline, the strongest pro-independence case, the strongest anti-independence / pro-federation case, the claim ledger, and the source list. It is designed for readers who want the practical civic question without partisan shortcuts.

## Current source-backed baseline

Clear question and clear majority are legitimacy tests, not just slogans. Current sources do not reduce every scenario to one fixed percentage or magic wording.

## Main argument map

- **Pro-independence case:** The strongest pro case is that a direct question and decisive result could create a democratic mandate that other governments would need to treat seriously.
- **Anti-independence / pro-federation case:** The strongest caution is that unclear wording, thin margins, or disputed public understanding could weaken any claimed mandate.
- **Uncertainty label:** High; clarity depends on wording, result, turnout context, and political/legal judgment.

## How to read the reports

Use the neutral report for the plain-language baseline. Use the pro and anti reports to test the strongest versions of each side. Use the claims and sources pages to inspect what is source-supported, what is inference, and what needs future rechecking.

## What to watch

- Official source updates.
- Court, legislative, fiscal, treaty, or policy changes.
- New credible expert analysis.
- Claims that need source support before they should be treated as settled.
